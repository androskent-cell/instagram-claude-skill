# Captions

## The window that matters

In the feed, Instagram shows only the first line or two (roughly 125 characters) before "… more". Most people decide there. So:

- The first line is a hook, not a greeting, not hashtags, not an emoji wall.
- Put the specific thing (number, price, result) inside those first ~125 characters.
- `scripts/caption_check.py` prints exactly that window so you can see it.

## Shape

```
Line 1: hook (≤ 125 characters)
Lines 2-6: the value, short lines, one idea
Last line: ONE call to action
Hashtags: 3-5 specific ones, at the end
```

## Calls to action that work (pick one)

| Goal | ES | EN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads by DM | «Comenta GUÍA y te la mando por mensaje.» | "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM it to you." |
| Bookings | «Reserva en el enlace de mi perfil.» | "Book from the link in my bio." |
| Saves | «Guárdalo para cuando lo necesites.» | "Save this for later." |
| Sales | «Escribe PRECIO y te paso todo.» | "DM me PRICE for the details." |

A keyword CTA needs an automation behind it, see [automations.md](automations.md).

## Hashtags and search

- 3-5 hashtags that describe the post and the audience (#inglesparaprincipiantes, #realtormiami), not giant ones (#love, #instagood).
- Write the words people search in the caption itself (Instagram search reads captions): "profe de inglés en línea", "manicura en Bogotá".
- Location tag when the business is local.

## Spanish-specific

- Neutral Latin American Spanish, tú. Use «en línea» or «online», and avoid Spain-only words (vale, móvil, ordenador) unless the user is in Spain.
- Opening ¿ and ¡ are required. Prices with the local currency symbol.

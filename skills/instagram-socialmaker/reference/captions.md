# Captions

## The window that matters

In the feed, Instagram shows only the first line or two (roughly 125 characters) before "… more". Most people decide there. So:

- The first line is a hook, not a greeting, not hashtags, not an emoji wall.
- Put the specific thing (number, price, result) inside those first ~125 characters.
- `scripts/caption_check.py` prints exactly that window so you can see it.

## Shape

```
Line 1: hook (≤ 125 characters)
Lines 2-6: the value, short lines, one idea
Last line: ONE call to action
Hashtags: 3-5 specific ones, at the end
```

The price goes in the post. "DM for price" loses sales everywhere, and in Brazil and France it breaks consumer law. A keyword CTA needs an automation behind it, see [automations.md](automations.md).

## Hashtags and search

- 3-5 hashtags that describe the post and the audience (#inglesparaprincipiantes, #realtormiami, #imoveiscuritiba, #ongleslyon), not giant ones (#love, #instagood). Instagram allows 5 at most. No accents in hashtags.
- Write the words people search in the caption itself (Instagram search reads captions): "profe de inglés en línea", "manicura en Bogotá".
- Location tag when the business is local.

## Spanish-specific

- Neutral Latin American Spanish, tú. Use «en línea» or «online», and avoid Spain-only words (vale, móvil, ordenador) unless the user is in Spain.
- Opening ¿ and ¡ are required. Prices with the local currency symbol.

## Portuguese-specific

Brazilian Portuguese with você, «pra» and «a gente»; prices «R$ 49,90», times «18h»; no ¿ or ¡; the price visible in the post; #publi when a brand pays. Everything else: [portuguese-brazil.md](portuguese-brazil.md).

## French-specific

tu or vous chosen once and kept; a space before ? ! : ; prices «45 € TTC», visible without asking; «Publicité» or «Collaboration commerciale» when a brand pays; sober tone, one exclamation mark at most. Everything else: [french-france.md](french-france.md).
